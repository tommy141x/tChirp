tChirp reproduces one small piece of real patrol car behaviour: when a siren is **park killed** — silenced automatically because the car has been put in park while still in pursuit mode — the car chirps at intervals until someone gets back in the front seat.

It is a reminder, not a siren controller. tChirp never turns a siren on or off. It watches whichever ELS your server already runs, notices that a siren was live when the officer left, and then makes that car chirp.

It is **free**, MIT-licensed and not escrowed. There is no encrypted file in it.

## What it is not

- **Not an ELS.** It adds no lighting, no siren control and no tones of its own. It needs an existing siren system, or the plain GTA siren.
- **Not a per-player sound.** The chirp is played from the vehicle, positionally, on every client near it. Walking away makes it quieter.
- **Not a siren tone library.** The sounds are native GTA sound names, or a soundset from a resource you already have.
- **Not standalone.** It needs [tLib](/docs/tLib) v0.8 or newer, which is free.

## Upgrading from 1.0.0

Every option was renamed in 1.1.0 to carry its unit, and **your existing `config.lua` keeps working**. tLib's config validator copies each old value onto its new name, uses it, and prints one line per rename naming the replacement. Nothing resets to a default.

<Callout type="warning" title="One 1.0.0 setting never did anything">
`Config.LVCResourceName` is documented in the old GitBook page and was not read by 1.0.0 either — LVC is detected from an event, which arrives under its own name whatever the resource is called. 1.1.0 reports it as retired at startup instead of ignoring it. See [Configuration](/docs/tChirp/configuration#settings-that-are-gone).
</Callout>
