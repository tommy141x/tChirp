tChirp reproduces one small piece of real patrol car behaviour: when a siren is **park killed** — silenced automatically because the car has been put in park while still in pursuit mode — the car chirps at intervals until someone gets back in the front seat.

It is a reminder, not a siren controller. tChirp never turns a siren on or off. It watches whichever ELS your server already runs, notices that a siren was live when the officer left, and then makes that car chirp.

It is **free**, MIT-licensed and not escrowed. There is no encrypted file in it.

## What it is not

- **Not an ELS.** It adds no lighting, no siren control and no tones of its own. It needs an existing siren system, or the plain GTA siren.
- **Not a per-player sound.** The chirp is played from the vehicle, positionally, on every client near it. Walking away makes it quieter.
- **Not a siren tone library.** The sounds are native GTA sound names, or a soundset from a resource you already have.
- **Not standalone.** It needs [tLib](/docs/tLib) v1.0 or newer, which is free.

## Upgrading from 1.0.0

1.1.0 renamed six options to carry their unit and carried your old values across for that one release. **2.0.0 does not.** A 1.0.0 `config.lua` still loads, but each old key is now reported at boot as a setting nothing reads, and the new key falls back to its shipped default — including `ChirpAssignments`, where the default is "nothing chirps".

<Callout type="danger" title="Check your config before upgrading to 2.0.0">
Lowercase the first letter of the six renamed keys. The full mapping, and what each one silently falls back to, is in [Configuration → Renamed, and no longer carried over](/docs/tChirp/configuration#renamed-and-no-longer-carried-over).
</Callout>

`Config.LVCResourceName` is documented in the old GitBook page and was not read by 1.0.0 either — LVC is detected from an event, which arrives under its own name whatever the resource is called. See [Configuration](/docs/tChirp/configuration#settings-that-are-gone).
