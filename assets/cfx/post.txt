# tChirp — the park-kill chirp, on whatever ELS you already run

tChirp reproduces one small piece of real patrol car behaviour. When a siren is **park killed** — silenced automatically because the car was put in park while still in pursuit mode — tChirp chirps that car at a randomised interval until somebody gets back in the front seat.

It is a reminder, not a siren controller: tChirp never turns a siren on or off. It watches whichever ELS your server already runs, notices that a siren was live when the officer left, and chirps the car they left.

## Features

- **Reads the ELS you already have** — tELS, Luxart Vehicle Control, LVC Fleet, ELS-FiveM, JLS and Advanced Lighting System, cascaded in that order, or the plain GTA siren if you run none of them
- **Nothing to operate** — no keybind, no menu, no command an officer uses. The behaviour is the product
- **Runs with zero edits** — every one of the eight options ships with a default, and a key you delete comes back as the value that shipped
- **The front seat is the rule, both ways** — leaving a front seat with the siren on arms the chirp; taking a front seat clears it immediately, for everyone
- **Per-model sounds** — a vehicle chirps the native sound you assign it, or a soundset from a resource you already have. A model you do not list never chirps
- **Free, and readable** — MIT licensed, every file in the download editable, no encrypted file and no escrow

---

## How it decides

Three things have to be true at the moment a player leaves a vehicle: they were in a **front seat**, the vehicle's **siren was on** as of the last check before they left, and the vehicle's **model is one you assigned a sound to**. Then the car goes on the chirp list.

The siren is sampled every 500 ms while you are seated and the exit is decided against the **previous** sample, because killing the siren is part of a park kill — by the time the player is out of the seat every provider correctly reports the siren as off, so a check taken at that moment would return false every single time and nothing would ever chirp.

Each chirp draws a fresh interval and a fresh duration from the configured ranges, so a row of parked cars does not fall into step.

> **A hand-killed siren can still chirp.** A siren switched off by hand and then left within the same 500 ms window still counts as a park kill, and nothing can tell the two apart afterwards — both look like "the siren was on a moment ago and the player is now outside".

---

## What it is not

- **Not an ELS.** It adds no lighting, no siren control and no tones of its own. It needs an existing siren system, or the plain GTA siren
- **Not a siren tone library.** The sounds are native GTA sound names, or a soundset from a resource you already have
- **Not a per-player sound.** The chirp is played from the vehicle with `PlaySoundFromEntity`, so every client near the car plays it positionally and it gets quieter as you walk away
- **Not usable without tLib.** tLib is free, and tChirp refuses to start below the version it declares rather than failing halfway down a file

---

## Getting it running

Three steps, and none of them is a purchase.

1. Drop `tChirp` into your resources folder, next to `tLib`. The folder must be named exactly `tChirp` — its exports are published as `exports.tChirp` and a FiveM resource is addressed by its folder name
2. `ensure tChirp` after `tLib`
3. Assign a sound to the vehicle models your department actually runs. It ships with the eight base-game police models, which is a starting point rather than your fleet

Nothing else is configured. `Config.sirenSystem` defaults to `auto`, which cascades through every provider — pin it to one only if you run exactly one ELS and want a wrong answer to be loud.

**Upgrading to 2.0.0?** The `tChirp:` event spellings are gone — lowercase `tchirp:` is the only form, and the verbs are unchanged. Rename every `tChirp:` name in your code: a stale listener never runs and a stale trigger goes nowhere, silently. Config is unaffected; options renamed in 1.1.0 are still copied onto their new names by tLib's validator, which prints one line per rename.
